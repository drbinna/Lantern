See what’s happening before anyone arrives.

Lantern pairs 360° camera stations with docked drones. When a camera flags activity, the closest drone flies out on its own and streams RGB and thermal video to security, while the camera keeps watching.

What happens when a camera sees something

The campus at rest. Camera stations watching, drones docked on a rooftop near the center. 0:00 A camera station flags a person in the arena lot after hours. 0:10 The closest drone launches from its dock and flies itself there. 1:30 Live RGB and thermal video in the security office. The camera keeps watching.

A camera station flags activity

360° stations watch lots, perimeters and quiet corners around the clock. When one sees a person, vehicle or heat source where there shouldn’t be one, it raises an alert. Blue-light phones, alarms and 911 calls can trigger it too.

The closest drone goes to look

Lantern picks the nearest drone with the battery to make the trip. Its dock opens and it flies itself to the spot, so no officer has to leave the security office to launch it.

Security sees it live, even in the dark

RGB and thermal video reaches the security office and every officer’s phone, while the fixed camera keeps watching the wider area. Often it shows that nothing is happening at all. Then the drone returns to its dock to charge.

Starting on campus. Built for any large property.

We’re starting with campus security at Oral Roberts University in Tulsa. ORU sits inside the Class D airspace of Tulsa Riverside Airport, so our first phase flies the full detect-to-return workflow with a certified pilot keeping the drone in sight, under FAA airspace authorization.

Full autonomy comes next, once we hold a waiver to fly beyond visual line of sight, backed by ADS-B receivers on every drone, ground-based radar, and parachutes for flights over people.

The same system fits anywhere the grounds are large and empty at night and a closer look is worth something before anyone drives out.

Lines we don’t cross

  • Identify people.No facial recognition, no biometrics. Not configurable, not available.
  • Decide someone is a threat.Cameras flag people, vehicles and heat where none should be. A person on your team looks at the video and decides what it means.
  • Fly without a reason.Drones don’t sweep the campus on a schedule. They stay docked until a camera, sensor or alarm gives them one, and return when the look is done.
  • See indoors.It gives you the building, the entrances, the exits and the approach. It does not give you the hallway.
  • Look where you've said it can't.Residence hall windows, the health center, the neighbors' property. Drones won’t fly there and cameras mask them out, enforced in software, not in a policy binder.
  • Keep footage it doesn't need.Camera and flight footage with no incident deletes itself on a short clock you set. Every view is logged.

One system. A drone for every site.

Every Lantern drone runs the same software, answers the same camera alerts and returns to the same dock. The airframe is chosen to fit the site.

Sentinel: campus security and police departments. Prototype flying indoors.

Quiver (by Arrow Air, open source): farms and ranches. Evaluating.

Ranger: winged VTOL for mines and large industrial sites. Concept.
