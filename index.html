What happens when a call comes in

The campus at rest. Aircraft docked on a rooftop near the center. 0:00 Blue-light call from the lot by the arena. 0:14 Launched from the nearest dock. Flying itself there. 1:30 Live video to dispatch and every unit on the way.

Something raises the alarm

A 911 call, a blue-light phone, a panic button, a door alarm. Lantern never decides on its own that something is wrong. It responds to the alarms your campus already has.

Dispatch launches with one action

The dispatcher sends the aircraft to the address without leaving the console. The nearest dock opens, the aircraft launches, and it flies itself there — around trees, around buildings.

Everyone en route sees the same picture

Live video reaches dispatch, incident command, and the phone of every responding officer. Which building. Which door. Who is outside it. Often, that nothing is happening at all.

What it will not do

  • Identify people.No facial recognition, no biometrics. Not configurable, not available.
  • Decide someone is a threat.No automated weapon or behavior detection. A person looks at the video and makes the call.
  • Patrol.The aircraft does not fly on a schedule or watch the campus. It leaves the dock when there is a call and returns when the call is over.
  • See indoors.It gives you the building, the entrances, the exits and the approach. It does not give you the hallway.
  • Fly where you've said it can't.Residence hall windows, the health center, the neighbors' property. Exclusion zones are enforced in the flight software, not in a policy binder.
  • Keep footage it doesn't need.Non-incident flights delete themselves on a short clock you set. Every view is logged.

Legible to the people it flies over

Every campus running Lantern publishes a flight log. Not the video — the count, the reason, how long the aircraft was up, and whether the ground response was cancelled after the aircraft arrived. That last number is usually the one that wins over a skeptical room.

We provide a use-policy template written for shared governance, and we'll sit in the meeting where it gets debated.
